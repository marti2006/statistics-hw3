Penetration process: LLN, variance and CLT

n systems, m attackers, i.i.d. increments X1, X2, … We study the total score Sn, the average score Sn/n and the normalized total score Sn/√n under the Bernoulli and Rademacher models. All simulations run in the browser.

0 The model

Each attacker j = 1,…,m attacks systems 1,…,n in sequence; the increment Xi(j) is the score obtained on system i. An attacker's trajectory is a random walk Sk(j) = X1(j) + … + Xk(j). The m attackers are m independent trajectories: the Monte Carlo sample used to estimate the distributions.

ModelValuesμ = E[X]σ² = Var(X)Meaning
Bernoulli(p){0, 1}pp(1−p)1 = system breached, 0 = attack failed
Rademacher{−1, +1}01+1 progress, −1 repelled: symmetric case

By independence E[Sn] = nμ and Var(Sn) = nσ². This gives the theoretical predictions:

QuantityMeanVarianceAs n → ∞
Snnμnσ²the variance diverges
Sn/nμσ²/n→ μ in probability (LLN)
Sn/√n√n μσ²if μ = 0: → N(0, σ²) in distribution (CLT)

In the Bernoulli model Sn/√n has mean √n p → ∞: it must be centred before applying the CLT, (Sn − np)/√(np(1−p)). This is why the Rademacher model is introduced: it has zero mean, so Sn/√n converges directly to N(0,1).

2 Distributions as n grows

n ∈ {10, 100, 1 000, 10 000}, m = 5000 attackers. The Monte Carlo error of a frequency is at most 0.5/√m, small enough to distinguish the effects of interest. Histogram bins are aligned to the lattice of attainable values, so no binning artefacts appear. For Sn each curve is normalized to its own peak, to compare position and width.

  • Sn shifts (mean np) and spreads out (σ√n): it has no limiting distribution.
  • Sn/n concentrates on μ until it becomes a spike: convergence in probability.
  • Sn/√n: in the Rademacher model the shape settles on N(0,1) from about n = 100. In the Bernoulli model the width stays constant (σ = √(p(1−p))) but the distribution drifts like √n p.

3 Variance under the different normalizations

Var(Sn) = nσ², Var(Sn/n) = σ²/n, Var(Sn/√n) = σ²: on a log-log scale these are lines with slope +1, −1 and 0.

  • Dividing by 1 is too little: the variance blows up.
  • Dividing by n is too much: the variance collapses and only the constant μ survives (LLN scale).
  • Dividing by √n is the critical scale: the only one with finite, non-zero variance, hence the only one with a non-degenerate limit in distribution (CLT scale).

4 Law of Large Numbers

For every ε > 0, P(|Sn/n − μ| > ε) → 0. By Chebyshev's inequality, P(|Sn/n − μ| > ε) ≤ σ²/(nε²). The probability is estimated as the fraction of attackers (m = 5000) that deviate from μ by more than ε. ● simulated · ⋯ Chebyshev · ‒ ‒ normal approximation 2(1 − Φ(ε√n/σ))

Fraction of attackers with |Sn/n − μ| > 0.05:

  • For every ε the probability goes to 0. The smaller ε, the more systems are needed: n ≫ σ²/ε².
  • Chebyshev holds but is pessimistic: it decreases like 1/n, while the true probability decreases exponentially (Hoeffding).
  • The Rademacher model (σ² = 1) converges more slowly than the Bernoulli model (σ² = p(1−p)): more noise means more systems are needed.

5 Central Limit Theorem (Rademacher)

With μ = 0 and σ² = 1: Sn/√n → N(0,1) in distribution. First row: histogram and Gaussian density. Second row: QQ-plot against the N(0,1) quantiles.

The Kolmogorov distance supx|Fn(x) − Φ(x)| measures the convergence. Berry–Esseen guarantees that it is ≤ 0.4748·E|X|³/(σ³√n) = 0.4748/√n. The exact curve can be computed because (Sn + n)/2 ~ Bin(n, 1/2).

  • Mean 0 and variance 1 hold for every n. What converges is the shape: excess kurtosis exactly −2/n, P(|Z| > 1.96) → 5%.
  • The distance decreases like 1/√n (slope −1/2) and stays below Berry–Esseen. The lattice with step 2/√n explains the staircase QQ-plot at n = 10.
  • For large n the simulated curve flattens at the Monte Carlo noise 1.36/√m: with finite m smaller distances cannot be observed.
  • The standardized Bernoulli also converges to the normal, a little more slowly because it is skewed.

6 The role of the number of attackers m

m as precision: the error of the estimates scales like 1/√m. The KS distance cannot fall below the true distance (≈ 0.0126 at n = 1000, lattice effect): beyond that point n must grow, not m.
m as threat: in the zero-mean case the best attacker reaches about √(2 ln m)·√n.

7 Interpretation

System size n

  • Sn is the absolute damage. It grows like nμ, with uncertainty σ√n. The relative uncertainty σ/(μ√n) decreases: on large fleets the outcome becomes predictable.
  • Sn/n is the compromise rate. It settles on the intrinsic effectiveness μ. With n ≳ σ²/ε² systems the estimate has precision ε and individual luck cancels out.
  • Sn/√n is the scale of the fluctuations. In the symmetric case it is Gaussian: |Sn| > 1.96√n happens only 5% of the time without a systematic advantage. A value beyond this threshold is evidence of μ > 0, which is the principle of a statistical test.

Number of attackers m

  • As statistical replication, m does not change the limit theorems, only the precision with which we observe them (∝ 1/√m).
  • As a threat, the aggregate score has mean mnμ and fluctuations √(mn)σ. Even with μ = 0, the best attacker reaches ≈ √(2 ln m)√n: with 3000 attackers about 3.5√n, a value a single attacker would reach with probability ≈ 2·10⁻⁴. Growth in m is only logarithmic, however, while growth in n is √n.
